# Sawa Stories v2: stories you want to finish

Design for the next version of "Stories for you" (graded readers in /app). Written 2026-10-23 for Sherif's review.
Status: design + 2 sample episodes. Nothing here is built yet; v1 (104 one-off stories, live in Sawa Lens 0.19.x) keeps working meanwhile.

## The problem with v1, in one line

v1 stories are good lessons but weak habits. Each is a one-off about a topic, many heroes are teenagers, and nothing pulls the reader back tomorrow. Our buyers are 25+, working, A2–B1, and their real enemy is not difficulty but the start-quit cycle (38% of Skool pain mentions). So the reader has to be built like a series people binge, not like a textbook.

## What the research says a reader should do

| Research finding | What the stories do about it |
|---|---|
| Start-quit cycle, boredom (38%) | Short serial episodes that end on a cliffhanger, so tomorrow already has a reason. A missed day never punishes: "Previously…" in two lines and you are back in. |
| "Remind me / رجعوني" (the most-liked pain) | The story itself is the reminder: "Will Nour get the job? Episode 4 opens tomorrow." A push or Home card with the cliffhanger line, not a guilt message. |
| The trigger is a job (211 mentions, top goal) | Heroes with their problems: an English interview, a first job at a multinational, night shifts with foreign customers, a Gulf contract, a freelance client abroad. |
| Knowing is not using | Every episode has one "phrase you can steal": a sentence the hero uses at work ("Could you say that again, please?"), with a 30-second "say it like Nour" voice prompt (optional, uses the existing speaking checker). |
| Shame, fear of sounding "fafy" | The heroes are scared too, make mistakes, freeze, and still win. Nobody in the stories laughs at an accent and gets away with it. |
| Community is what works (60% of Skool posts are check-ins) | Finishing an episode makes a ready "My day" check-in ("Day 12 · I read Episode 5 of The Interview"). Optional class vote on the hero's choices. |
| No proof of progress | Each season ends with "Then → now": the % of words you knew in episode 1 vs the finale, and the words you collected on the way. |
| No time | Episodes are 3–4 minutes on a phone, about 250 words at A2 and 400 at B1. Listening counts as reading. |

## The new format

### 1. Seasons, not single stories
- A **season** = 8 episodes about one hero, one goal, one problem. One season per level to start: A2 and B1.
- **Episodes unlock one a day** (recommended), with up to 2 extra on weekends. Every episode ends on a hook; the hook line is shown on Home tomorrow.
- **"Previously on…"**: 2 lines at the top of every episode, so a learner who disappeared for a week is back in 20 seconds (comeback mode, not punishment).
- v1 stories stay as **"Quick reads"**: the one-offs by interest, for when you want something different.

### 2. Heroes like them
Age 22–35, Egyptian or Arab, working or looking for work, with family and money pressure, the way the audience describes their own lives. They are learning English too. Their English gets better during the season, and the reader's does with them.

Season ideas (for Sherif to pick 2–3):
1. **The Interview** (A2): Nour, 27, an accountant in Tanta, gets an interview at a multinational in Cairo. The interview is in English, and she freezes when she speaks. (Sample below.)
2. **Night Shift** (B1): Karim, 31, takes UK customer calls at night in Smart Village and wants to become team leader. A strange caller knows things about him. (Sample below.)
3. **The Gulf Contract** (A2–B1): Hassan, 29, an engineer, gets a contract in Dubai: a new city, an English-speaking site and a manager who talks fast.
4. **The Client** (B1): Mariam, 25, a freelance designer in Alexandria, gets her first client in Canada. Emails, calls, a deadline and a disaster.
5. **Lost in Istanbul** (A2): two friends on their first trip abroad. Their luggage is lost, and they have to sort it out in English.

Interests stay: each season is tagged with quiz topics (business, tech, travel, football…), and side characters bring in hobbies (a hero who plays football on Fridays, a sister who raps).

### 3. Choices (light branching)
Two or three times a season, the reader decides: "What should Nour say?", with two short English options. The choice changes the **opening paragraph** of the next episode (two versions), then the story comes back to the same path. That makes it feel personal without doubling the writing.
- Option A (recommended to start): **personal choice**. Each learner picks; we keep it.
- Option B: **class vote**. Everyone votes; the majority decides tomorrow's episode for everyone, with the result shown ("63% chose B"). This is social and great for Skool posts, but slower to build.

### 4. Language built into the story
- 5–6 new words per episode (i+1), underlined, with Arabic meanings and ＋ to review cards (as now).
- **Words come back**: words from episode 1 appear again in episodes 3 and 6. That is spaced repetition inside the story, so reading the season is review.
- **Phrase you can steal**: one work-life phrase per episode, highlighted. There's an optional 30-second "say it like Nour" recording, checked by the existing speaking checker.
- **Quick check, friendlier**: 2 "what happened" questions, 1 word question and 1 "what would you do?" question with no wrong answer, instead of 4 test questions.

### 5. Audio
- The natural voice, recorded once per episode and shared (already built; switch it on in Settings).
- **Listen first** mode: hear the episode with the text hidden, then read it. That's listening practice for "I can't follow natives".
- Later: two voices for dialogue (narrator + hero).

### 6. Phone-first reading
- 3–4 minutes, one column, big text, tap a word for its meaning, press and hold to save a phrase (as now).
- **Resume where you stopped**, and a thin progress bar per episode.
- The cliffhanger line closes the episode as a full-screen card ("Tomorrow: Episode 4 · *The phone rings again.*").

### 7. Saving words to Language Lens
Already shared: words saved in a story go to the same review cards as YouTube and Language Lens. New: a season word list ("37 words from The Interview") to review before the finale.

### 8. The Silk Road
- Rawi the storyteller (built) tells "the next episode" instead of a random story.
- Each finished episode gives a small story item in the game (Nour's CV, Karim's headset). Collecting a season's items unlocks a cosmetic (the Storyteller's scarf).
- The season finale can be a special Silk Road quest: "Help Nour prepare", with a fight whose questions are the season's words.

## Sample episodes (the new style)

### Sample 1 · A2 · *The Interview*, Episode 1: "The Email" (≈280 words)

> **Previously:** (first episode, no recap)

Nour is twenty-seven. She is an accountant in a small company in Tanta. The salary is low, the office is hot, and her boss shouts a lot. Every night, she looks at jobs on her phone. Every good job says the same thing: *English required.*

On Thursday, at 11 p.m., she sees an email. It is from Hartwell, a big international company in Cairo. “Dear Nour, thank you for your application. We would like to invite you to an interview on Sunday at 10 a.m.”

Nour reads it three times. Then she reads the last line again. *The interview will be in English.*

Her heart beats fast. She understands English when she reads it. She can watch a video with subtitles. But when she speaks, her mind goes white. At school, a teacher laughed at her accent once, in front of the whole class. She still remembers his face.

Her little brother, Ziad, sees her phone. “An interview? In English? You?” He laughs and goes back to his game.

Nour closes the door of her room. She opens her notes and writes one sentence in English: *Could you say that again, please?* She says it out loud, quietly. Then again, a little louder. It sounds strange, but it is her voice.

She practises until 1 a.m. She writes small answers: her name, her job, why she wants to work at Hartwell. She is tired, but for the first time in a long time, she feels something new. Hope.

Then her phone rings. It is a number from London. Nour looks at it. It rings again.

She answers. “H-hello?”

“Hi, is this Nour? This is Emma from Hartwell. I’m so sorry, but can we move your interview… to tomorrow morning?”

**What should Nour say?**
- A) “Yes, tomorrow is fine.” (Episode 2 opens: Nour has nine hours to get ready.)
- B) “Could we keep Sunday, please?” (Episode 2 opens: Emma pauses, and then says something surprising.)

- **New words:** application, invite, required, accent, practise, hope
- **Phrase you can steal:** *Could you say that again, please?*
- **Say it like Nour (optional):** record 3 sentences about your job (30 seconds).
- **Quick check:** Where is the interview? · Why is Nour afraid to speak? · What does “required” mean? · *What would you do: tomorrow or Sunday?* (no wrong answer)
- **Tomorrow:** Episode 2: "Nine Hours".

### Sample 2 · B1 · *Night Shift*, Episode 1: "The 3 a.m. Caller" (≈420 words)

> **Previously:** (first episode, no recap)

At 2:55 a.m., the call centre in Smart Village is half asleep. Forty people in headsets answer calls from Britain, where it is still evening. Karim, thirty-one, has done this job for four years. He knows the script by heart: *Thank you for calling Northline, my name is Karim, how can I help you today?*

He is good at it, mostly. His English is clear, his manager says, but “too careful”. When customers speak fast or get angry, Karim freezes for a second, and that second feels like an hour. Next month, the company will choose a new team leader. Everyone says it will be Karim or Dalia. Dalia never freezes.

At exactly 3:00 a.m., his line lights up.

“Good evening,” says an old man’s voice, slow and polite. “Am I speaking to Karim?”

Karim blinks. Customers never ask for him by name. Calls are random. “Yes, sir. How can I help you today?”

“My name is Arthur Harris. I have a question about my internet bill.” The question is simple, and Karim fixes it in two minutes. But Mr Harris doesn’t hang up. “You sound tired, young man. Do you like this job?”

The script has no answer for that. Karim laughs nervously. “It’s… a good job, sir.”

“That isn’t what I asked,” Mr Harris says kindly. Then he says goodnight and ends the call.

The next night, at 3:00 a.m. exactly, the line lights up again. “Karim? It’s Arthur. I have another small question.” And the night after that. Each time, the question is easy, and each time, Mr Harris asks one personal question at the end: about Karim’s family, his dreams, the first English word he ever learned. Strangely, Karim starts to look forward to 3 a.m. With Mr Harris, he never freezes.

On the fifth night, Dalia leans over the desk. “Who do you keep talking to? The system says those calls come from a number that doesn’t exist.”

Karim checks his screen. She is right. The caller ID is just a line of zeros.

At 3:00 a.m., the phone lights up.

“Karim,” says Mr Harris, and this time his voice is serious. “I know about the job offer in Dubai. Please listen to me carefully. Don’t take it.”

Karim stops breathing. He hasn’t told anyone about Dubai. Not his manager, not Dalia. Not even his mother.

- **New words:** script, freeze, random, polite, look forward to, caller ID
- **Phrase you can steal:** *Let me check that for you, it’ll just take a moment.*
- **Say it like Karim (optional):** answer an imaginary customer in 3 sentences.
- **Quick check:** Why does Karim's manager call his English "too careful"? · What is strange about the calls? · What does "look forward to" mean? · *Would you tell Dalia about Mr Harris?* (no wrong answer)
- **Tomorrow:** Episode 2: "Zeros".

## What it costs and how it gets made

- **Writing:** seasons are written in Claude sessions, like v1, at no API cost. 8 episodes is about one working session per season. Sherif reads and approves each season before it goes live (the admin "Hide" button already exists).
- **Voice:** OpenAI TTS for about $0.01–0.02 per episode, recorded once and shared. About $0.15 per season.
- **AI per student:** none. Personal stories stay optional and off.

## Build plan (after Sherif's decisions)

1. **Data:** the story JSON gets `season`, `episode`, `previously`, `choice` ({q, options: [{label, opening}]}), `phrase` ({en, ar}), `next` (the hook line) and `returning` (words that come back). Old files keep working.
2. **App (/app Learn → Stories):** a season shelf, the episode page with the "Previously" box, the phrase card, the choice card and the full-screen cliffhanger card, resume where you stopped, and listen-first mode.
3. **Home:** the cliffhanger line as tomorrow's card. An optional push later.
4. **Check-in:** a ready "My day" text after each episode (the copy button first; Skool posting stays manual).
5. **Silk Road:** Rawi tells the next episode, story items, and later a season-finale quest.
6. **Content:** the first two seasons (A2 *The Interview*, B1 *Night Shift*), 8 episodes each.
7. **Fix v1:** rewrite the teenage heroes in the Quick reads to adults where it fits (content only).
